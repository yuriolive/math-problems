## Complex projective packings / Grassmannian frames (the "Game of Sloanes" leaderboard)
**Screen: reject** | reallyOpen=True alreadyTaken=True | q1checker=True q2gradient=False q3gap=False q4reach=True

- **Objection:** Question 2 is a NO, not a YES, and the candidate inverted it. The score is mu = max over n(n-1)/2 pairs, so at a generic configuration exactly one pair is active and the gradient w.r.t. the other n-2 vectors is identically zero; at any near-optimal configuration the active set is large and no single-vector perturbation descends. Two independent sources confirm this is binding: pending PR #15 on the leaderboard says it uses a smoothed surrogate "rather than subgradient methods on the true max, which stall on the non-smooth ridge where multiple pairs are simultaneously near-extremal"; and peer-reviewed Roca-Jerat & Roman-Roche, Mach. Learn.: Sci. Technol., 5 Aug 2025 (doi 10.1088/2632-2153/adf53d, the jrr cells) report that "minimizing the coherence is not the best way to yield frames with low coherence" because the energy "becomes insensitive to some of the optimization parameters", and substitute Riesz s-energy. The gradient lives on a surrogate, not on the objective the checker scores - the Erdos-64 failure mode in floating-point costume. Consequence: cuda-search is the wrong instrument (all three current record-setters use log-sum-exp annealing + L-BFGS-B multistart, a few hundred lines of CPU scipy), the RTX 4070 Super buys nothing, and Lean - the repo's strongest asset - has zero surface on a float array. Compounding this: the candidate scraped the stale 2026-04-21 README and missed six open PRs claiming 37+ cells, including dustinmixon's 26-cell "sol" batch (#14, 19 Jul 2026), djma's 8 cells explicitly beating #14 (#15, 23 Jul), and yhua's 3 cells beating both (#16, 4 Sep 2026, five days ago). Three of the candidate's four named target cells are already beaten there (d=6 n=41 -> 0.429327051576; d=5 n=49 -> 0.540083591144; d=7 n=29 -> 0.340545293579). The headline "d=7 n=33..48, sixteen untouched AUTO cells" also fails: the legend defines AUTO as deletion beating "any of the submitted packings", i.e. submissions lost, and the exact analogous d=5 plateau (5x35/36/37/38, all 0.499988903350) was harvested by three independent parties in seven weeks using the publicly documented plateau heuristic that PR #15 spells out. Finally the deliverable is not a result: the 8th-decimal rule rewards convergence depth, with accepted margins of 6.81e-9 (PR #12, its own author calling it "microscopic") and 4.07e-7 (#14) - tighter convergence to the same critical point, which is the definition of a calibration exercise. - **statement:** Given d and n, find n unit vectors phi_1..phi_n in C^d minimising the coherence mu(Phi) = max_{1<=j<k<=n} |<phi_j, phi_k>|. Equivalently: pack n lines through the origin of C^d so the pairwise angles are as large as possible. The leaderboard tracks best-known mu for all 2<=d<=7 and d+2<=n<=49, plus sporadic (d,n) up to d=19, n=361.

- **objectiveShape:** Real-valued, continuous, non-smooth (a max of |inner products|), and strongly gradient-bearing: perturbing a single vector by epsilon moves mu by O(epsilon) whenever that vector realises the max. The only real work is rigour: do it twice, once in f64 for speed and once in interval or MPFR arithmetic to certify the 8th decimal, and differential-test the two (exactly the verifier/ + tests/ pattern this repo already has). Hard limit of the checker: none that binds. Memory per configuration is 2dn f64 = 5.5 KB at d=7,n=49, so a 12 GB RTX 4070 Super holds ~2M candidate configurations resident; the tracked target range needs 686 to 13718 doubles per configuration. Compare with the Erdos-64 failure: there the checker's ceiling (62 vertices) sat below the live target (78); here the ceiling is ~4 orders of magnitude above it.

## Best-known L-infinity star discrepancy point sets in dimension 3-6
**Screen: reject** | reallyOpen=True alreadyTaken=True | q1checker=False q2gradient=True q3gap=True q4reach=False

- **Objection:** Q4 fails, and it fails the same way Erdos-64 did — the candidate compares the wrong two numbers. For a SEARCH target the binding limit is objective evaluations per second, not whether one exact evaluation is feasible.

Numbers in the same units. Repo instrument: ~8e6 verified evaluations/sec (CUDA SA, 4070 Super). Required at the target corner: exact D*_inf costs O(n^(1+d/2)); at d=6, n=200 that is 200^4 = 1.6e9 box operations per evaluation. Calibrating against the published timing in arXiv:2306.16998 Table 1 (d=7, n=300 ~ 160 s, i.e. 300^4.5 ~ 1.4e11 units / 160 s ~ 9e8 units/s) gives ~1.8 s per SINGLE evaluation, i.e. ~0.5 evals/sec. Shortfall: ~10^7. And DEM is GPU-hostile — recursive box decomposition, dynamic branching, per-thread working sets, no bitmask encoding — so porting it to the 4070 does not recover seven orders of magnitude. The instrument named in the candidate ("cuda-search") is mismatched to its own checker.

Primary-source confirmation from the group that BUILT the parallel DEM implementation, on the candidate's exact instances (Clement/Doerr/Paquete, arXiv:2306.15276, J. Complexity 2024; they run d in {4,5,6,8,10,15,25} with n in {100,150,200,250}): in d=6, "from n>=120 and n-k>=30 onwards, both _BF instantiations are often (or always for DEM_BF) unable to finish a single run" under a 1-hour cutoff; in d=3, "DEM_BF does not finish even once for n=200 and k=130". The candidate's target is n=20..200, d=3..6. The exact checker already breaks down inside the target box, for experts, on better hardware for this workload.

The candidate's stated limit ("n<=750 in d=6; n<=200 in d=8; n<=50 in d=10", attributed to arXiv:2306.16998) I could NOT confirm — that paper is not a parallel-DEM reach paper, it is a black-box-optimization benchmarking paper, and what it actually says is that DEM "performs reliably well for up to a few hundred points in dimension 8, or dozens in dimension 10", a statement about one computation. So the Q4 line in the candidate is both misattributed and measuring the wrong quantity.

Q1 also fails at d>=4. Exact star discrepancy is NP-hard (Gnewuch/Srivastav/Winzen 2009) and W[1]-hard in d (Giannopoulos/Knauer/Wahlstrom/Werner, J. Complexity 2012). A one-day Rust job is real for d=2 (O(n log n) anchor-box grid scan) and defensible for d=3 (naive n^4 grid); correct DEM in d=4..6 is a research artifact. Worse, the proposed differential reference (brute force O(n^(d+1))) is itself infeasible at target size: d=6, n=200 is 200^7 ~ 1.3e16. So the exact checker would run at target size with NO independent cross-check — precisely the trust failure tools/intake/README.md says every false published figure in this repo came from.

The fallback (heuristic TA inside the loop, exact DEM only for final verification) is worse than no search. TA "always outputs a lower bound for the discrepancy", so an annealer minimizing it overfits the estimator and reports point sets whose TRUE discrepancy is higher — a fake record by construction. And arXiv:2306.16998's headline finding is that black-box optimizers are bad at computing the sup at all ("random search outperforms even the more sophisticated solvers"), so the inner objective is itself an open hard problem.

Q2 passes but not cleanly. D*_inf is real-valued, continuous, piecewise-smooth and perturbation-sensitive in aggregate, so it is not a flat lexicographic integer objective and SA does work on it in the literature — the candidate is right that this is not the Erdos-64 failure mode. But the critical box is determined by at most d point coordinates, so an epsilon-perturbation of any of the other n-d points leaves the score EXACTLY unchanged: at n=100, d=3 roughly 97% of single-point epsilon-moves are exactly flat. And Clement/Kirk/Owen/Rusch (arXiv:2508.04926) state verbatim it "is not differentiable with respect to the input points, which makes it impossible to optimize via gradient-based methods" and switch to L2. Rescuable with large moves or an L_p surrogate — but the surrogate is exactly what the SOTA groups already do, and using it means you are no longer optimizing the tracked quantity.

Openness holds up (this part of the candidate is honest). Proven optima only d=2 n<=21 and d=3 n<=8 (Clement/Doerr/Klamroth/Paquete, arXiv:2311.17463, PAMS Ser. B 12 (2025) 78-90, peer-reviewed); everything else is a best-known upper value with no matching lower bound. Not a proven optimum, not settled.

Taken-ness is understated. Verified NEGATIVE for the letter of the exclusion list: AlphaEvolve's 67 problems do not include it (code search over google-deepmind/alphaevolve_repository_of_problems returns only #40, the Erdos discrepancy problem on +/-1 sign patterns, an unrelated object, plus incidental van der Corput mentions); OpenEvolve's example directory listing has no star-discrepancy entry; code search over org:baidu-baige (LoongFlow) returns nothing relevant. It also DOES state its verification: exact in 2D ("we compute D_N* exactly by scanning extremal anchor boxes on the coordinate-induced grid (overall O(N log N))"), threshold-accepting approximation in 3D, validated against published 2D optima for N<=20 and 3D optima for N<=8 to 1e-6. ## Putatively optimal coverings of S^2 by n equal caps (covering radius)
**Screen: NOT REACHED (workflow stopped)**

- **statement:** For each n, find p_1..p_n on the unit sphere S^2 minimising the covering radius rho(P) = max_{x in S^2} min_i arccos(<x,p_i>) - i.e. the smallest angular radius such that n congruent caps centred at the p_i cover the sphere. This is the covering dual of Tammes (which maximises the minimum pairwise distance).

- **openStatus:** Open for essentially all n. The Hardin-Sloane-Smith library at https://neilsloane.com/coverings/ ("A Library of Putatively Optimal Coverings of the Sphere with n Equal Caps", R. H. Hardin, N. J. A. Sloane, W. D. Smith, copyright Feb 1994, page last modified 31 Oct 2015) covers n = 4..130 and is explicitly labelled putatively optimal - obtained by numerical optimisation, not proven. Optimality is known only for the three degenerate cases n=4, 6, 12 (tetrahedron, octahedron, icosahedron vertices); for d>1 there is no general formula and the optimal configuration's structure depends on n. Companion mirror with per-n data, plots and symmetry groups: https://randomwalk.de/sphere/caps/ (n=4..50, rho from ~70.53deg at n=4 down to ~18.30deg at n=50); it warns its own coordinates are visualisation-accuracy approximations and points back to Sloane for high precision.

- **statement:** Let A ⊆ {1,…,N} be such that at most one integer n has more than one representation n = a+b with a ≤ b ∈ A (i.e. Sidon apart from a single exceptional sum). Two concrete deliverables: (i) extend the exact values of F(N) beyond N = 69, the current computational frontier; (ii) find A with |A|/√N > 2/√3, which would answer Erdős and Freud's question in the negative.

- **openStatus:** Open. erdosproblems.com/864 status OPEN (https://www.erdosproblems.com/864). Exact values are known only for N ≤ 69: David Spencer computed 69 terms (teorth/erdosproblems issue #143, closed, https://github.com/teorth/erdosproblems/issues/143, output at https://github.com/davidspencer6174/erdos_problem_scripts/blob/master/erdos_864_output.txt), and he notes the sequence is not in the OEIS. The problem thread has two comments and no numeric improvement to either published bound (https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/864).

- **currentRecord:** Lower bound F(N) ≥ (1+o(1))(2/√3)N^{1/2} ≈ 1.154700·√N, Erdős–Freud, J. Number Theory 1991, MR 1111371 (peer-reviewed) — the same reflected-Sidon construction as #840. Upper bound F(N) ≤ (√2+o(1))N^{1/2} ≈ 1.414214·√N, Desmond Weisenberg, erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/864, 11 Aug 2025 — FORUM CLAIM, short and checkable (split A at the exceptional sum n into A∩[1,n/2] and A∩(n/2,n], both genuinely Sidon, then apply h(N) ~ √N from #30), but not peer-reviewed and not in the site's main text. For the search itself use the penalty form: at a fixed target size m, minimise E(A) = (number of sums n with cnt[n] ≥ 2) − 1, clipped at 0. E is an integer in [0, m²/2] that changes by O(m) on a single-element swap — dense, no plateau. Across N the reported figure |A|/√N is real-valued with resolution 1/√N. Contrast with Erdős #64, where most degree-preserving edge swaps left every tier of the (C4,C8,C16,C32) profile unchanged.

- **checkerSketch:** Rust, a couple of hours. Allocate cnt: Vec<u16> of length 2N+1; for each pair a ≤ b in A increment cnt[a+b]; accept iff #{n : cnt[n] ≥ 2} ≤ 1. O(|A|²) = O(N) per full evaluation, and incremental add/remove is O(|A|) = O(√N). Differential-test against a naive HashMap reference and, crucially, against Spencer's published 69 published terms and witness sets — a ready-made regression fixture, which is the kind of independent ground truth the repo's #64 history says to insist on. HARD LIMIT vs TARGET, same units: cnt array is 2N u16s = 4N bytes, so N = 10^8 costs 400 MB (host) and N = 10^9 costs 4 GB — inside the 12 GB GPU. Exhaustive/SAT limit is far lower: choosing ~1.15√N elements from [1,N] means the exact-value frontier is in the low hundreds, not millions. TARGET: exact values N = 70 … ~300 via SAT/branch-and-bound (current frontier 69); heuristic lower-bound records for N up to 10^6 via CUDA. Both inside the checker's limits with large margin.

Claim (ii) (the real content, from which (i) follows by adding one edge): if e(G) = floor(n^2/4) + floor((n-1)/2) then either G contains a saturated planar subgraph on m >= 4 vertices, or G is the join T_{n1} + n2*K1 of a tree on n1 vertices with an independent set of size n2, where 0 <= n1 - n2 <= 2. The planarity-free reformulation, which is the one to state in Lean: if G on n vertices has e(G) >= floor(n^2/4) + floor((n-1)/2) and G is not of the form T_{n1} + n2*K1, then G contains a copy of K4 or a copy of C_ell + 2K1 for some ell >= 3 (both of which are saturated planar: K4 has 4 vertices and 6 = 3*4-6 edges; C_ell + 2K1 has ell+2 vertices and 3*ell = 3(ell+2)-6 edges).

- **openStatus:** The problem is listed as PROVED on erdosproblems.com (https://www.erdosproblems.com/1019), but the *proof* is not in the literature. Terence Tao read the only published candidate, Simonovits [Si75] 'On graphs not containing large saturated planar graphs' (1975), and stated on 18 Oct 2025 that it 'does not quite solve #1019 as stated' — it needs m sufficiently large, and it leaves the auxiliary problem unresolved, promising a sequel that never appeared. Thomas Bloom then wrote (19 Oct 2025) 'I think now that we've seen [Si75], the problem should be marked as unsolved'. The status was only restored to PROVED after Stijn Cambie corresponded directly with Simonovits and posted a one-page reconstruction of Chapter 9 of Simonovits's (untranslated, Hungarian) thesis as a forum comment on 21 Nov 2025: https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/discuss/1019. This is exactly the situation the repo already exploited once for #64/jul059.

- **currentRecord:** Erdős [Er69c] (Math. Nachr. 1969, MR 270968) proved only the weaker statement that floor(n^2/4)+k edges force a saturated planar subgraph on >> k/n vertices. Erdős [Er71, p.102] (MR 0277392) states it is 'easy to construct' a graph with floor(n^2/4)+floor((n-1)/2) edges and no saturated planar subgraph on >3 vertices, so the threshold in the claim is sharp. Simonovits [Si75] (MR 379295, 1365-1386) is the only published work in the line and, per Tao, does not settle m=4. The induction base cases in Cambie's argument are n <= 4, far inside every limit. ## Erdős #834 — Ruiliang Li's resolution of the Erdős–Lovász 3-critical 3-graph problem (unrefereed arXiv preprint with a machine-checkable certificate half)
**Screen: NOT REACHED (workflow stopped)**

- **statement:** Two independent claims, corresponding to the two readings of '3-critical' that Erdős and Lovász left unspecified. (A) Transversal reading: if H is a 3-uniform hypergraph with transversal number tau(H) = 3 and tau(H - e) = 2 for every edge e, then H has at most 10 edges; consequently delta(H) <= 6, and the bound is sharp, witnessed by the complete 3-graph K5^(3). (B) Chromatic reading: there exists an explicit 3-uniform hypergraph H on 9 vertices with weak chromatic number chi(H) = 3 and minimum degree delta(H) = 7 such that deleting any single edge or any single vertex makes H 2-colourable. Together these answer Erdős and Lovász's question 'does there exist a 3-critical 3-uniform hypergraph in which every vertex has degree >= 7?' with 'no' under (A) and 'yes' under (B).

The author's own abstract says the criticality of the 9-vertex example is 'certified by explicit witness 2-colourings listed in the appendices, together with a short verification script' — i.e. the correctness of half the paper currently rests on an unpublished script nobody has re-run. Raphael Steiner had already observed on the problem page that the transversal reading makes the problem 'a finite computation'.

- **currentRecord:** The problem itself is from Erdős [Er74d, p.282] and had stood unanswered since 1974 under either reading. Li25 (arXiv:2512.24850) is the only claimed resolution; the sharpness witness K5^(3) is elementary. The chromatic-reading answer is a single 9-vertex object, so 9 is by construction the record order, and the transversal-reading edge bound of record is 10 with delta <= 6. No competing bound exists in the literature. No prize attached in problems.yaml.

- **objectiveShape:** Flat, and in the strongest possible sense: part (B) is a single fixed finite object, so there is no objective at all, only a decision. Part (A) is a universally quantified bound over bounded-size objects. - **checkerSketch:** Ground truth is genuinely small and fully inside every limit. Part (B): the object lives on 9 vertices, so the ambient edge universe is C(9,3) = 84 triples; a 2-colouring is a map Fin 9 -> Bool, of which there are 2^9 = 512. Checking 'H has no proper 2-colouring' is 512 evaluations; checking 'chi(H) = 3' adds one witness 3-colouring; checking criticality is |E(H)| + 9 further instances, each of which the appendix already supplies a witness colouring for, so each is a single evaluation rather than a 512-way search. Total worst case under 100,000 primitive Boolean tests — orders of magnitude inside `decide` in the Lean kernel, no `native_decide` needed, and trivially inside a Rust bitmask checker (represent each triple as a u16 mask over 9 bits; the whole verification is microseconds). Compare the repo's stated hard limit — the 64-vertex uint64[64] bitmask engine — against the target: 9 vertices versus 64, a factor of 7 of headroom, so unlike #64 this problem is entirely reachable. Part (A) is not a finite search in the same way (the vertex set is unbounded a priori), but Li's own argument bounds it: at most 10 edges means at most 30 vertices, so once the structural reduction is in place the residual case analysis is over 3-graphs with <= 10 edges, which a Rust orderly-generation checker can enumerate up to isomorphism as a differential test against the Lean proof. The Lean layer needs its own hypergraph definitions (Mathlib has no hypergraph library — nothing under Mathlib/Combinatorics for 3-uniform hypergraphs, tau, or weak colourings), but for a fixed 9-element vertex type `Finset (Finset (Fin 9))` with a `DecidablePred` instance is about 40 lines.

- **takenBy:** No automated or AI attack found on the formalization. erdosproblems.com shows #834 with 'Proof expositions (0)' and formal_status: unformalized; the reactions panel lists nobody under 'Working on formalising'. Not in Palomar (https://palomar-registry.org). google-deepmind/formal-conjectures contains statement-only files for many Erdős problems; I did not confirm a file for 834 either way. Nothing in LoongFlow or OpenEvolve examples concerns critical hypergraphs.

- **currentRecord:** No published audit of this corpus exists that I could find, so there is no record to beat, only a baseline to establish. Adjacent facts for calibration: Palomar was announced 18 Aug 2026 (Tao, 'Palomar — a registry of Lean verified mathematics', https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/08/18/palomar-a-registry-of-lean-verified-mathematics/) and checks proofs and publishes exact statements, but it postdates the great majority of these 298 entries and registration is not retroactive; the Formalisation thread also records that Lean 4 Web links, the previous mechanism for making a formalization checkable without downloading it, 'die pretty soon, if something with the website or Lean version changes'. google-deepmind/formal-conjectures deliberately formalizes statements only, not proofs.

- **objectiveShape:** Graded, and this is the only candidate here that is. Each audited problem contributes a discrete pass/fail/conditional verdict, so coverage is a monotone integer score that rises with every problem processed — a local increment always moves it, unlike the four theorem targets above. That makes it schedulable and partially deliverable at any moment, which is the opposite of the all-or-nothing shape of a single formalization. It is not a search objective and no annealer or evolutionary loop applies.

- **checkerSketch:** The checker already exists in this repository: tools/lean/audit.py, the `#print axioms` pass that formalization-egc/AxiomCheck.lean already runs over every theorem in the strict-2/3 development. Scale it out: a harness that per problem pins a toolchain and Mathlib revision, does `lake build`, greps for `sorry` and for `axiom` declarations, emits the axiom set of the named theorem, and diffs the formal statement against the problem text. Hard limit versus target, in the same units: the binding resource is Mathlib build time and disk, roughly 15-60 minutes and several GB per distinct toolchain/Mathlib pin, against 298 problems — so the honest ceiling on a single machine is on the order of 10-30 audited problems per day once caching by toolchain is in place, i.e. two to six weeks of wall-clock for full coverage, and considerably less if you start with the 44 pre-2026 entries and the 5 self-declared conditional ones. Failure mode to guard against, and it is the same one that produced this repository's own false results: a build that errors out must be recorded as 'not checked', never as 'passed'. That is exactly the short-circuit bug the #64 verifier had, where untested cycle tiers were serialized as `false` and read as 'absent'.

- **instrument:** lean-proof

- **takenBy:** No prior automated attack found, and the absence is itself evidenced: Bloom's own August 2026 post describes checking a claimed formalization as manual work he would like to outsource to Palomar, and Alexeev's February 2026 post lists his own conditional proofs from memory rather than from a tool. Palomar performs this function for newly registered claims going forward, not for the existing corpus, so the two are complementary rather than competing. Nothing in LoongFlow or OpenEvolve is remotely adjacent. Caveat on novelty: because Palomar exists and is being pushed as the default, this window is closing — the audit is most valuable in the next few months and much less so once the corpus has migrated.

- **whyTractable:** This is the candidate that uses the repository's single most distinctive asset — the mechanical axiom audit — for what it is actually best at, and it needs no new mathematics to produce its first result. Sizing: the harness is 300-600 lines of Python plus CI, a few days. Each conditional gap then costs whatever its mathematics costs, and here the estimates diverge sharply and I want to be blunt about it: the four PNT+-dependent problems (#205, #648, #678, #862) are not weeks-scale if you intend to prove the analytic inputs, because that is the PNT+ project itself; they ARE weeks-scale if the goal is the strictly narrower and still-worthwhile one of *wiring in* the already-formalized PNT+ statements so the results become unconditional, which is the software-engineering task Alexeev explicitly declined and estimated as possible. #659's Bernays axiom is the genuinely uncertain one: Alexeev himself says one of the two needed ingredients 'is not available'. So the deliverable I would actually commit to is the audit plus the PNT+ wiring, not a promise to discharge every axiom. Confidence: high that the harness works and produces a citable, novel result; medium on how many conditional formalizations exist beyond the five self-reported ones; low on whether any individual axiom is dischargeable in weeks.
